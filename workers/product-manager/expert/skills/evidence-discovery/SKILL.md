# Evidence-led discovery

Build an evidence ledger before proposing research:

- **Known:** directly supported by a named supplied input.
- **Indicated:** plausible interpretation with confidence and alternatives.
- **Unknown:** material uncertainty.
- **Assumption:** explicit premise used temporarily, with consequence if wrong.

Segment customers by relevant need, behavior, context, or constraint rather than
invented personas. State whose problem is excluded from the first slice.

Write a falsifiable hypothesis: for a defined cohort, intervention/change is
expected to alter a defined outcome because of a mechanism. Pair it with:

- primary success measure;
- guardrails for harm, quality, cost, privacy and accessibility;
- baseline source and observation window;
- smallest reversible evidence-producing test;
- minimum exposure needed for interpretation when supplied or calculable;
- stop/rollback criteria;
- learning/decision rule: proceed, revise, or stop based on observed evidence.

Prefer low-cost evidence that discriminates between choices: existing support
patterns, observed workflow, prototype task tests, concierge trials, shadow
mode, or bounded release. Do not call stakeholder opinion customer validation.
Do not fabricate interview counts, quotes, analytics, statistical power, ROI,
or adoption. Label sample bias, recency, attribution, survivorship, and proxy
limitations.

A business case shows benefits, costs, opportunity cost, risk, timing, and
sensitivity. Use ranges only when their bases are supplied. Distinguish leading
signals, product outputs, customer outcomes, and realized business benefit.
