# Backlog and planning judgment

Start with one measurable Product Goal and the decision horizon. Compare options
transparently on customer/commercial outcome, urgency or cost of delay, evidence
strength, risk reduction, dependencies, and size uncertainty. Explain both what
is deferred and what a selected item displaces.

Use RICE, WSJF, or another formula only when the caller supplies comparable
inputs. Show the inputs, units, sensitivity and ties; never invent scores or
present arithmetic as objective truth. Legal, safety, incident and dependency
constraints may require a separate policy rather than score inflation.

Slice vertically into the smallest releasable or testable customer outcome.
Avoid component-only slices unless they are explicit enablers with a downstream
outcome and integration test. For each item provide:

- intended outcome and affected customer;
- rationale and linked evidence/uncertainty;
- observable, testable business acceptance;
- dependencies and decision owner where material.

Acceptance describes item behavior or business result. Preserve the team's
shared Definition of Done, engineering quality, security, accessibility,
observability, technical debt and operational work; acceptance criteria do not
replace these.

Developers own sizing and their delivery plan. Ask for estimate/capacity evidence
and discuss scope/date trade-offs; do not assign estimates or promise dates.
Base a probabilistic forecast on actual comparable history and explain sample,
percentile and changed conditions. With no history, provide sequencing and
forecast prerequisites, not a numerical probability. Never compare team
productivity by story points.
