# Vector illustration craft

## Interpret before drawing

Reduce the brief to one primary read and no more than a few supporting reads. Identify the subject, action, emotional register, audience, required explicit lettering, and nonnegotiable style cues. Separate facts supplied by the brief from visual invention. Make three tiny composition concepts mentally or on the canvas; choose the one with the clearest silhouette and strongest subject/background relationship.

When no style is supplied, choose and record one direction because it reinforces the subject—not because it is easiest. When style is supplied, convert its adjectives into concrete constraints for shape language, edge character, detail density, palette, contrast, and rhythm. A style constraint must visibly affect the drawing.

## Select a method

- **Editorial flat / geometry:** simplify to distinctive silhouettes and a few confident planes. Use controlled shape repetition, offset alignment, limited value steps, and one visual surprise. Avoid generic icon arrangements and equal emphasis everywhere.
- **Organic botanical / engraving:** build directional stems, contour rhythm, tapered lobes, and clustered detail that follows growth. Use hatch paths sparingly to describe curvature and light; vary spacing and weight rather than scattering texture.
- **Isometric technical:** establish one consistent axis system and scale. Construct shared planes, overlaps, cutaways, and line weights coherently. Distinguish structure from accents; do not fake depth with incompatible angles.
- **Expressive painterly-vector:** use intentional irregular contours, broad interlocking color masses, broken edge rhythms, and selective gestural accents. Preserve a readable silhouette and value structure; “expressive” is not random wobble or excess nodes.

Hybrids need one dominant grammar. Do not apply all methods at once.

## Compose and draw

1. Mark safe margins and a focal zone. Allocate negative space as an active shape. Test whether the main silhouette still reads at thumbnail size.
2. Block three to five value groups before detail. Establish the focal contrast, then reduce contrast and detail in supporting regions. Use a compact palette with roles: field, dominant, shadow, light, and restrained accent. Check that neighboring forms differ in value as well as hue.
3. Build back-to-front. Use overlap, occlusion, scale change, and shared baselines to establish depth. Avoid accidental tangencies: either overlap clearly or separate clearly.
4. Place Bézier nodes at extrema and genuine direction changes. Use the fewest nodes that preserve the intended contour; align handles with the tangent and inspect for flat spots, hooks, and lumpy transitions. Stock circles and rectangles are construction aids, not a finished visual language.
5. Make form convincing with consistent light direction, turning planes, cast-shadow logic, curvature, and edge control. Reserve sharp edges and highest contrast for the focal area. Softer or quieter boundaries can merge subordinate forms.
6. Vary rhythm deliberately: alternate large/medium/small masses, clustered/open intervals, and calm/active passages. Texture must follow form or meaning. Remove unmotivated dots, stripes, labels, highlights, and micro-details.
7. Name layers by role such as `Background field`, `Subject silhouette`, `Depth and shadow`, and `Accents`. Name important groups. Keep gradients and clips minimal, locally referenced, and semantically organized.

## Critique loop

Query artwork bounds and read Inkscape diagnostics. Render the final exported SVG, not merely the master. Inspect at full size for curve craft, seams, clipping, edge quality, repeated artifacts, and unintended gaps. Inspect at thumbnail size for hierarchy, silhouette, value grouping, and visual noise. If image viewing is unavailable, do not infer that the render looks correct from a successful command.

Remove elements that neither clarify subject, space, style, nor rhythm. Check explicit text character by character in the editable master, then confirm the final contains outlines and no live text. Ensure simplification preserves identity rather than turning the work into generic clipart.

## Independent review rubric

Score each category 1–5 and reject professional-quality claims when any category is below 4:

- **Brief fidelity:** required subject, action, mood, details, dimensions, wording, and optional style are recognizably honored without invented facts or branding.
- **Composition:** focal hierarchy, silhouette, balance, depth, cropping, negative space, and thumbnail read are intentional.
- **Drawing craft:** curves, joins, tangencies, overlaps, light/shadow, edge control, palette, value structure, rhythm, and texture are controlled.
- **Style consistency:** shape language, marks, detail density, palette, and finish sustain the requested/chosen direction rather than a generic vector look.
- **Technical/editing quality:** named layers/groups, economical paths, resolved defs, editable master, outlined final text, vector-only portability, correct canvas, and clean Inkscape render.

The checker establishes technical structure and provenance only. It cannot establish semantic fidelity, originality, beauty, professional composition, or what the PNG looks like to a human. Those require independent full-size and thumbnail review.
