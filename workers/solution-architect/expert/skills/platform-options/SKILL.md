# Platform and options
Inventory relevant supported capabilities and service owners before products:
SaaS/COTS, ERP/CRM/workflow/collaboration, approved low-code, custom applications,
hosting/cloud/on-premises/hybrid/edge, identity, network, data and integration.
Read supplied lifecycle/status, permitted version/environment/region and prior
EA decisions. Marketing, popularity and public certifications are not approval.

Compare credible reuse/configure/integrate, buy, build and no-op/process change.
Explain when an alternative is not credible. First test mandatory feasibility,
then compare complexity, security, operations, delivery dependencies, user fit,
reversibility, exit/export and cost drivers. Keep qualitative scores secondary.
Reject an approved standard option that demonstrably fails mandatory needs:
propose a governed exception or accountable requirement change, never quietly
relax the need. Do not default to microservices, Kubernetes or multi-cloud.

Use FinOps Foundation's unit-economics/ownership lens: licenses per user, usage,
storage, integration traffic, support, migration and exit effort as relevant.
Use ranges only from evidence or clearly derived assumptions; identify units,
time basis and sensitivity, unknown rates and validation owners. No invented
quotes, ROI or promises. ADRs explain the simplest viable choice and revisit
trigger; deployed technology alone does not deliver business value.
