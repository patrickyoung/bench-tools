# Business outcomes and investment choices

Use when architecture direction must support a measurable business outcome or
choose among investments.

## Procedure

1. Restate the outcome in business terms: revenue, avoidable cost, retention,
   risk exposure, service quality, or time-to-value. Identify population,
   measure owner, timeframe, and decision horizon.
2. Record the baseline, target, and source only when supplied. Otherwise mark
   each unknown and ask for the decisive measurement rather than inventing it.
3. Write a causal hypothesis: intervention, affected behavior/capability,
   expected observable change, assumptions, confounders, and falsifying signal.
4. Compare alternatives including reuse, defer/do nothing, purchased/SaaS,
   platform extension, and replacement where relevant. Show opportunity cost,
   reversibility, dependency, uncertainty, and who holds the investment right.
5. Recommend the smallest evidence-producing commitment. Define leading and
   lagging measures, review trigger, acceptance threshold or decision rule, and
   what would cause stop/hold/scale.
6. Separate outputs delivered, capabilities adopted, behavior changed, and
   benefits realized. Assign measure collection and benefit accountability.

## Evidence

Use supplied financial records, service metrics, customer behavior, contracts,
validated research, operational data, and dated owner statements. Define
numerator, denominator, cohort, source, and period when possible. Label proxy
measures and causal uncertainty.

## Common failures

- Invented baselines, targets, prices, savings, ROI, or causal certainty.
- Technology adoption or artifact completion presented as an outcome.
- Benefits with no accountable owner or collection method.
- False precision from incomparable options.
- Ignoring the no-op and displaced investment.
- Treating a reviewable recommendation as budget authorization.
