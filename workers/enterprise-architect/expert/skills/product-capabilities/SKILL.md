# Product capabilities and portfolio

Use when the decision concerns capability ownership, platform consumers,
service promises, reuse, product/SaaS selection, lifecycle, or disposition.

## Procedure

1. Define stable capability IDs independently of today's org chart or software.
   Name outcome, consumers, accountable business owner, platform/product owner,
   service boundary, and dependencies.
2. Map current applications and platforms to capabilities. For each record:
   stable asset ID; users/consumers; owner; contracts; dependencies; cost or
   unknown; criticality; health; current lifecycle; evidence date; confidence.
3. Identify overlap, gaps, coupling, orphan ownership, unsupported dependencies,
   weak contracts, and concentration risk. Distinguish platform product,
   purchased/SaaS product, shared component, and local application.
4. Assess reusable platforms as products: consumer journey, discoverability,
   onboarding, service promises/SLOs, support, feedback, adoption friction,
   funding, and accountable lifecycle.
5. Recommend one disposition per asset: `invest`, `sustain`, `consolidate`,
   `replace`, `retire`, or `evaluate`. Keep this separate from current
   lifecycle. Give rationale, evidence, confidence, dependencies, acceptance
   gates, owner, and next review date/trigger.
6. For retirement, require accepted consumer migration, data retention/export,
   contract exit, operational support, dependency removal, and decommission
   evidence. A date alone is not a sunset plan.
7. For emerging products, start from a demonstrated gap; compare reuse and do
   nothing, remain vendor-independent, and define pilot plus adopt/hold/stop
   gates, portability, security, interoperability, and total-cost evidence.

## Decisions and evidence

Prefer durable capability boundaries and explicit contracts over duplicate
features. Evidence includes catalogs, owner attestations, usage/consumer lists,
contracts, incident/health trends, support status, cost records, dependency
maps, service measures, and dated review records. Mark absent data unknown.

## Common failures

- Calling a project or technology a capability without a durable outcome.
- Treating adoption as value or recommending a trend without a real gap.
- Combining current lifecycle and proposed disposition.
- Claiming savings from consolidation without migration and exit cost.
- Retiring before consumers, data, contracts, support, and dependencies pass.
- Building a new portal when an existing catalog needs better descriptors.
