name: executive-slidecraft
description: Use when pacing an executive decision deck with purposeful slides, crisp supported copy and native editable evidence under a shared publication narrative.
---

# Purpose before layout
For every slide, identify the reader question, supported point and relationship
that explains it. Use CONTRACT.md to retain purpose, chosen medium, design
rationale, notes and claim/message mapping; these are design requirements, not
new schema fields. Plan an intentional sequence of context, finding, explanation,
tradeoff and decision as the evidence needs, not a compulsory dramatic arc.
A defer decision must not become a victorious ending.

Use supported takeaway titles for findings and direct subject/scope titles for
context. Shorten without stripping decision-changing qualifications. One clear
purpose can have supporting detail, but multiple independent takeaways usually
need separation. Put technical depth in notes/appendix, not essential caveats.

# Match relationship and medium
Choose aligned criteria tables for exact comparisons, bound native charts for
quantitative evidence, small multiples for comparable views, and explanatory
diagrams only for supported relationships. Use imagery only when it clarifies
recognition, spatial meaning or a concept. Map each major illustrative element
to meaning and state its limits. No unsupported architecture, implied measured
topology, causal flow, delivery dates or approved roadmap.

Evidence must remain editable charts/tables/text in the deck, not a screenshot
of a chart or a visually convincing illustration with retyped values. Bind data
references and preserve chart semantics. Do not invent values, rank, precision
or confidence. If the host cannot provide native editability, report that gap
rather than silently substituting a raster.

# Compose a clear first reading
Use one obvious starting point and dominant relationship. Align and group by
meaning before adding boxes; repeat geometry for peers, arrows for real
direction, nesting for actual scope and position for evidenced order.
Apply the shared typography, hierarchy, palette and semantic emphasis.
Use negative space actively. Status needs words/symbols as well as color.

Vary rhythm for narrative function: a decisive statement, evidence, explanation
and comparison need not share the same layout. Avoid bullet dumps, repeating
card grids, ornamental graphics and novelty without explanatory benefit.
Keep copy crisp and leave air around evidence. Split overload rather than
shrink essential type. Choose legible sizes for the presentation setting and
available fonts, then rely on final render review, not inherited canvas units.

# Review the actual outcome through supplied evidence
Check slide-to-slide argument, visible caveats, notes and appendix against the
shared plan and source IDs. Make unknowns, score bounds, preference sensitivity
and confidence read differently. A large number or accent must not manufacture
priority, certainty or a stronger winner.

Use host-rendered previews and fresh controller-supplied critiques when available;
do not author critiques or claim images were seen from text alone. Correct
clipping, tiny labels, weak hierarchy, false visual implications and unsupported
imagery. Keep spec/master, inputs and source mappings for reproducible revision.
Successful deck creation establishes neither native editability nor visual craft
without the host's corresponding checks and complete inspection.
