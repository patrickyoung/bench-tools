Cross-platform anatomy of a durable digital worker

The agent persists. The execution is replaceable.

Bench does not preserve only a contract and a log. It externalizes the meaningful agent—definition, character, curated memory, skills, specialist homes, work, state, events, receipts, effects, and checks—so a fresh execution can continue it. Only live compute and process-local scratch are disposable. Each platform implements those durable layers differently.

The architectural click

Nothing important should live only inside the process.

Bench separates four domains. Three persist; one is replaceable. “Controller-owned” describes who may mutate authoritative definition or evidence—not what the worker can see, and not everything that survives.

Persists · governed definition

Who the agent is

Readable by the worker; mutable only through operator or controller paths.

  • GOAL.md, AGENTS.md, SOUL.md, plans and heartbeat
  • Curated MEMORY.md and reviewed skills/
  • Provisioned tools/ and nested agents/
  • Trusted bin/check and bin/wake
Persists · worker-writable

What the agent is doing

Durable continuity the worker may change, without turning it into authority.

  • work/ deliverables and working tree
  • state/ progress, plans, and file-shaped KV
  • Definition and action proposals that grant no power
  • Each specialist home’s own work and state
Persists · controller evidence

What happened

Written outside the confined worker and independently inspectable.

  • Replayable Ask events and Ply verdict receipts
  • Checkpoint and selected-skill records
  • Approval, action, amendment, and learning receipts
  • Explicit unknown effect state when no receipt is trustworthy
Replaceable · execution

What may disappear

The compute substrate is not the agent’s source of truth.

  • Foreground process tree or task VM
  • Heap, model-client objects, and open descriptors
  • Private temporary scratch and unflushed buffers
  • Any in-memory view reconstructible from durable records

A fresh execution is not a blank agent. It reloads the governed definition and procedures, reopens the same work and durable state, reconstructs the recorded request history, follows the named checkpoint, and continues under the same external check and authority. We replace compute; we do not discard continuity.

Memory is plural

Each kind of continuity needs a different writer.

Calling all of this “memory” erases the trust model. Curated facts, learned procedures, operational state, conversation history, and evidence should not share one writable bucket.

Honest deltas

Bench is not “all of that, but smaller.” It chooses a different center.

The external platforms mostly center a managed agent, knowledge system, graph runtime, or business workflow. Bench centers the durable obligation and treats any particular execution as replaceable.

Bench establishes unusually explicit semantic seams

  • An admitted, content-addressed outcome contract separate from the worker.
  • An independently executable bin/check that runs outside the model-authored command boundary.
  • A digest-verifiable, replayable normalized request-event history.
  • One-shot human authorization bound to exact proposal bytes and current state.
  • An explicit status 125 for an external effect whose outcome is unknown—never an automatic retry.
  • Reviewed procedural learning derived from replay-verified fail→pass recovery.
  • Unix replaceability: models, tools, retrieval, policy, memory, and protocols stay ordinary narrow programs.

The larger platforms establish capabilities beyond Bench

  • Managed global or enterprise fleets, scaling, revisions, traffic, queues, and distributed durability.
  • Workload identity, IAM, SSO/SCIM, network policy, DLP, audit, compliance, and organization governance.
  • Managed enterprise retrieval, content connectors, knowledge graphs, grounding, and ingestion pipelines.
  • Visual/no-code authoring, generated UIs, business channels, RPA, and human workflow.
  • Dataset evaluation, experiments, red teaming, dashboards, production monitoring, and review apps.
  • Dev/test/prod application lifecycle, catalogues, cost controls, and managed deployment.
  • Google code/browser/container sandboxes, Foundry VM-isolated sessions, and eligible Glean per-session shell/Python isolation—stronger managed execution surfaces than Cage today.

Method and caveats

A design map, not a feature score.

The comparison is deliberately about ownership of guarantees. A checkmark would hide the most consequential distinction: a platform can expose an approval button or trace viewer without binding the decision or evidence to the exact obligation being judged.

How to use this page

Start with the recipe nearest your operating center. Data-heavy teams may begin with Databricks; Google or Foundry can anchor a managed code-first fleet; Copilot, Glean, Writer, or Thunk can drastically shorten business application delivery; Lang* maximizes framework composability; Bench makes the semantic control plane explicit.

Then add only the obligation-plane seams your risk requires. A drafting assistant may not need an effect ledger. A financial action agent probably needs canonical approval, idempotency, reconciliation, and an independent verifier.
